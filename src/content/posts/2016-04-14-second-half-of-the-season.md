<div class="wp-content">
<p>How lovely it was to start the second half<br />
of the season with a band as good as Robin Mason&rsquo;s Apex Jazz &amp; Swing<br />
Band.   After weeks of jazz starvation<br />
this was the equivalent of gourmet fine dining.<br />
The mix of jazz and swing works so well and from where I sat I saw heads<br />
nodding and shoulders swinging.</p>
<p></p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="516" data-orig-width="473"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_o5m9rs32XM1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="516" data-orig-width="473" /></figure>
<p>The opening number of &lsquo;You are my Sunshine&rsquo;<br />
was an inspired choice for the Chase Jazzmen with snow and ice outside.  The selection throughout the programme was<br />
good and the tempo ranged through fast, bosa nova to slow.  The band was well received.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="447" data-orig-width="643"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_o5m9sj55sJ1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="447" data-orig-width="643" /></figure>
<p>Jazz Buddies with Arthur Brown and Matt<br />
Palmer gave a first-class performance on 24 January with a good mix of music to<br />
make your toes tap.  The musicians played<br />
smoothly together.  I would like to<br />
especially mention the trombonist, Mike Owen, who was so good.  Singing was undertaken in the main by Matt<br />
Palmer (reeds) with his usual style.  A<br />
rhythym section consisting of Brian Mellor, Dennis Mowatt and Tim Jones<br />
completed the band.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="463" data-orig-width="650"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_o5m9t7TBrd1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="463" data-orig-width="650" /></figure>
<p>On 31 January it was the turn of Bear Town<br />
Stompers.  This band was originally<br />
formed to play in a Jazz &amp; Blues Festival but is now a well established<br />
band performing easy-going, traditional jazz.<br />
I thought from time to time it had a gentle quality to the music which<br />
is so often missing.  Long time member,<br />
Jill Dodds, presented the SJS Musician of the Year Trophy to Peter ‘Pedro&rsquo;<br />
Robinson at the conclusion of the first half.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="498" data-orig-width="440"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_o5m9ts0jSV1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="498" data-orig-width="440" /></figure>
<p>February 7th saw our first visit by Jake<br />
Leg Jug Band with Mike Pemberton (trombone).<br />
Neil Hulse played guitar and offered up vocal renditions, Esther Brennan<br />
on washboard and ukelele also sang, and there were Toby Wilson on banjo and<br />
dobro and Duncan Wilcox on double bass, all joining in with the vocals.  Now this was something different … not only a<br />
band comprising fairly young musicians but with a distinct southern<br />
influence.  It was a real treat for the<br />
ears with a refreshingly different repertoire.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="623" data-orig-width="836"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_o5m9ukw8qu1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="623" data-orig-width="836" /></figure>
<p>The Old Fashioned Love Band visited again<br />
on 14 February.  As always the band<br />
played with such verve and musicality they were the joy we have all come to<br />
expect.   Their music was a wide range of<br />
foot-taping jazz standards and the band featured, Mike Pemberton, Harry<br />
Forelius, Jake Reeves and Mo Green.<br />
Wonderful.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="353" data-orig-width="496"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_o5m9v0thkG1rp3l2g_540.png" data-orig-height="353" data-orig-width="496" /></figure>
<p>Annie Hawkins Funky Butt Hall Jazzband made<br />
a welcome appearance on 21 February.<br />
Annie brought with her an all-star band to match her smiling<br />
talents.  The band consisted of Michael<br />
Hogarth on piano, Bob Rowbotham on banjo, Barry Wood on Drums, Liz Bacon on<br />
clarinet, Pete Wright on cornet and Jeff Milner on trombone.  As always this concert was well received.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="623" data-orig-width="834"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_o5m9vulNbY1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="623" data-orig-width="834" /></figure>
<p>Last concert in February saw the Frog<br />
Island Jazz Band with us.  This band<br />
plays classic and authentic New Orleans-style music from the 1920s and<br />
1930s.  The band has been awarded the<br />
accolade ‘International Honorary Citizens of New Orleans&rsquo; and when you hear<br />
them you understand why.</p>
<p>March&rsquo;s first concert was Matt Palmer&rsquo;s<br />
Millennium Eagle Jazz Band.  This band continually<br />
attracts high ratings and Stafford SJS are always pleased to see them.  Baby Jools on drums was a treat, as was Brian<br />
Lawrence on double bass and sousaphone, Pete Brown on cornet, Terry Williams on<br />
trombone and Brian Mellor on banjo.  Many<br />
smiling faces left the club at the end of this show.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="620" data-orig-width="833"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_o5m9w6O1LD1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="620" data-orig-width="833" /></figure>
<p>On 13 March there was an appearance by The<br />
Sopranos, featuring Chris Pearce and Andy Leggett.  This band has recently been formed by these<br />
two top reedsmen.  The rest of the band<br />
consisted of Dave Deakin on trombone, Ken Wright on drums, Tony Sharpe on<br />
double bass and Phil Probert on Guitar.<br />
Best summed up as easy listening by such competent musicians.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="406" data-orig-width="570"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_o5m9wm2cLu1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="406" data-orig-width="570" /></figure>
<p>Last week was the turn of Wabash<br />
Jazzmen.  With Mike Challinor on banjo<br />
&amp; guitar, Mike Pemberton on trombone, Mike Hayler on reeds, Richard Vernon<br />
on string bass and Pete Ange on trumpet this band gave a performance to knock<br />
your socks off.  Come back soon, please.</p>
