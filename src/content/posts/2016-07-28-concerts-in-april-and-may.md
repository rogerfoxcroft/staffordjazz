<p>29<br />
May <b>– Jeff &amp; Anne Barnhart with<br />
Thomas &ldquo;Spats&rdquo; Langham and Graham Smith in &lsquo;Ragtime, Swing, Blues and<br />
Beyond</b>.  As last concerts go this<br />
went with a crash, bang, wallop.   It was<br />
a mixed bag with jazz from the 20s up , classical music and gospel music.  There were several numbers that very few<br />
people had heard before and there is always joy in listening to something for<br />
the first time.  The room was packed and<br />
the audience highly appreciative.  I<br />
might say that this is the only group we have had who got a standing ovation at<br />
the end of the first set … and that is saying something.   Musicians were Anne Barnhart on flute, Jeff<br />
Barnhart on piano, Graham Smith on the d4rums and Thomas &ldquo;Spats&rdquo;<br />
Langham on banjo and guitar.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="665" data-orig-width="886"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_ob17hiZK3o1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="665" data-orig-width="886" /></figure>
<p>22<br />
May saw the <b>Merseysippi Jazz Band</b>,<br />
who last came to SJS in June 1966 when we were based at the Gatehouse.  The musicians were:  John Higham, Malcolm Hogarth, Pete Fryer,<br />
Dave Dixon, Pete Darwin, Derek Vaux and Dave Rigby.  There was one number entitled ‘Wocka the<br />
Fish&rsquo;, which was enchanting, and another number about a chap who was with a<br />
large 20 stone girl and would draw chalk marks to show where he had come from<br />
and these crossed paths with a chap with a larger than life girlfriend coming<br />
the other way!  You couldn&rsquo;t make it<br />
up.   Such an enjoyable concert.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="677" data-orig-width="903"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_ob17i8KfQ91rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="677" data-orig-width="903" /></figure>
<p>15<br />
May – <b>Stafford Grammar School Big<br />
Band</b> (part of the Stafford Music Festival).  This was their first appearance at Stafford<br />
Jazz Society but with a reputation that preceded them following their highly<br />
acclaimed performances at last year&rsquo;s Stafford Music Festival and the Upton Jazz<br />
Festival.  Quite how successful they were<br />
is reflected by the fact that they were chosen as Band of the Year!</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="677" data-orig-width="903"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_ob17iqDHWs1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="677" data-orig-width="903" /></figure>
<p>8<br />
May – <b>Swingin&rsquo; Jim&rsquo;s Dance Band</b><br />
gave us a high performance, as always in the past, with a couple of new arrangements<br />
thrown in for good measure.  They are<br />
always assured of a warm welcome at SJS.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="705" data-orig-width="940"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_ob17jggc9S1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="705" data-orig-width="940" /></figure>
<p>1<br />
May – <b>Tame Valley Stompers</b> <b>– Terry&rsquo;s Dead Good Boys.</b>   Repertoire included songs from films from<br />
the early 1930s, most of which were instantly recognisable.   Terry played ‘Wonderful World&rsquo; on the<br />
trombone, which was really special.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="677" data-orig-width="903"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_ob17jyLDsy1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="677" data-orig-width="903" /></figure>
<p><b>Chicago Swing Katz</b> made an appearance on 24 April.<br />
From blues to boogie, this group really knows how to appeal across the board.   The banter gave an intimate feel to the<br />
whole show.   I applaud Derek Harris for<br />
sporting red braces to match his (and the rest of the band&rsquo;s) red tie.   There were Pete Ainge on trumpet, Mike<br />
Blakesley on trombone, Dave Margaroni on bass, Mike Carney on forurns, Derek<br />
Harrison on keyboard and vocals and Geoff Matthews on reeds.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="677" data-orig-width="903"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_ob17kffky71rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="677" data-orig-width="903" /></figure>
<p><b>Steve King Big Band</b> visited on 17 April.  This<br />
band was so professional both in performance and in appearance.   As a treat Steve King played on his trumpet<br />
for ‘Georgia On My Mind&rsquo; and Lynn Dawes gave some fine renditions, my favourite<br />
being ‘Hard Hearted Hannah&rsquo;.  Starting<br />
the second set ‘Honeysuckle Rose&rsquo; was lead into the full number with just the<br />
vocalist singing and the double bass providing the beat – just beautiful.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="677" data-orig-width="903"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_ob17kzuTpY1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="677" data-orig-width="903" /></figure>
<p>on<br />
10 April we welcomed <b>Matt Palmer&rsquo;s<br />
Rhythmic Reeds with Christine Tyrrell.</b><br />
Considering this band has only been in existence for a year it works<br />
wonderfully well.  The audience were<br />
jumpting in their seats with heads bobbing and feet tapping – always a good<br />
indicator of a band being well received.<br />
The musicians were Mark Challinor on banjo, Jim Switherton on double<br />
bass, Nick Ward on drums, George Huxley on reeds and Matt Palmer on reeds.   Christine Tyrrell gave us some great songs –<br />
she is always a favourite with us.</p>
<p>3<br />
April saw <b>Pete Allen&rsquo;s Reeds ‘n&rsquo;<br />
Rhythms</b>.  What a pleasure to<br />
welcome three talented musicians.   They<br />
played music garnered mainly from the 20s and 30s, including ragtime.  It is easy to get the decades muddled up as<br />
most of the really good tunes have been recycled again and again so seem so<br />
familiar to us.</p>
