<div class="wp-content">
<p>16 April –<br />
Willy&rsquo;s Weepers with Eric &ldquo;Busker&rdquo; Newton:  A favourite with SJS, this band gave us all a<br />
thoroughly good gig.  Terry Brunt,<br />
swathed in long scarf, was on trombone, Jack Cotterill on drums, Peter &lsquo;Pedro&rsquo;<br />
Robinson on banjo/guitar, Pete Brown on trumpet, John Mascett (apologies if<br />
this is wrong!) on double bass and the whole was led by clarinettist Eric<br />
‘Busker&rsquo; Newton.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="631" data-orig-width="845"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_oqg3kjJ7wt1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="631" data-orig-width="845" /></figure>
<p>9 April –<br />
Debonaires Little Big Band:  This was the<br />
band&rsquo;s first appearance at SJS and we were not disappointed.  It was lovely to have both male and female<br />
vocals throughout.  Max Debon on trumpet<br />
led with Sarah Eastwood (clarinet), Rick Halliwell (alto sax), Anne Holder<br />
(tenor sax), Austin Ralphson (trombone), Peter Wilson (piano), Peter ‘Pedro&rsquo;<br />
Robinson (bass) and Rob Turner (drums).<br />
Everyone will look forward to them coming back. </p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="617" data-orig-width="823"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_oqg3ktGODh1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="617" data-orig-width="823" /></figure>
<p>2 April –<br />
Kevin Grenfell&rsquo;s Jazz Giants:  No Richard<br />
Leach on this occasion but there was the addition of reeds player, Mike<br />
Wills.  Kevin Grenfell (trombone and<br />
trumpet) joked his way through the gig.<br />
Today saw the strangest brolly parade ever with one lone parader (? Sylvia)<br />
but joined half way through by regulars Heather and Nick.  Other members of the band included Jack<br />
Cotterill (drums), Wayne Elliott (bass) and Richard Hughes (piano).</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="617" data-orig-width="823"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_oqg3l3tzFc1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="617" data-orig-width="823" /></figure>
<p>26 March –<br />
Frog Island Jazz Band:  The room was<br />
festooned in flowers for Mothering Sunday and the band, who are honorary<br />
citizens of New Orleans, played numbers from<br />
the 20s and 30s.  John Whitehead<br />
led on cornet, with Jim Hurd (clarinet), John Jeanes (trombone), Rob Fullalove<br />
(brass bass), Brian Lawrence (banjo), Keith Durston I(piano) and Chris Marchant<br />
(drums)</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="637" data-orig-width="853"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_oqg3le9Ygo1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="637" data-orig-width="853" /></figure>
<p>The room at White<br />
Eagle Club looked lovely with vases of daffodils on the tables to welcome the<br />
Spring. </p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="628" data-orig-width="841"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_oqg3lkMrQ01rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="628" data-orig-width="841" /></figure>
<p>19 March –<br />
Sticky Wicket&rsquo;s Swingtet featuring Chris Gumbley:  Sticky Wicket, the drummer, gave SJS a<br />
glimpse of his flashing drumsticks.  He<br />
is widely regarded as the UKs number 1 swing drummer.  Playing along with him was Stafford&rsquo;s own top<br />
reedsman, Chris Gumbley, vocals were provided by Linda Morris, piano by Mike<br />
Kemp and Clive Morton was on double bass.<br />
A concert to remember.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="629" data-orig-width="839"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_oqg3lxe3ob1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="629" data-orig-width="839" /></figure>
<p>12 March –<br />
Steve King Big Band:  The room was awash<br />
with music with the coming on Steve King and his bang.   The audience was bigger than usual with<br />
standing room only for stragglers.  The<br />
20 piece band was joined by the very talented US vocalist, Lynn Dawes.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="630" data-orig-width="840"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_oqg3mminCm1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="630" data-orig-width="840" /></figure>
<p>5 March –<br />
New Orleans Heat:  This 7-piece band did<br />
what it says on the label – that is they played old New Orleans.  This is one of the hottest bands on the<br />
circuit and play across Europe.  All<br />
musicians were hugely talented but I was particularly taken with the trumpeter<br />
– Richard Church – who I understand collects trumpets and has 33 to date!  The rest of the band consisted of leader<br />
Barry Grummett on piano, Dave Vickers (trombone), Roger Bird (clarinet &amp;<br />
alto sax), Harry Slater (bass), Colin Bushell (drums) and Alan Birkinhead<br />
(banjo).</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="632" data-orig-width="846"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_oqg3nhqYuw1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="632" data-orig-width="846" /></figure>
<p>26 February<br />
– Robin Mason&rsquo;s Apex Jazz &amp; Swing Band:<br />
A pacy blues number burst into the room and washed over an enthusiastic<br />
audience at the start of the concert.<br />
During the following two hours the music ranged from big band to trad,<br />
with Latin thrown into the mix for good measure.  Whatever does it for you, from blues to<br />
swing, was there.</p>
