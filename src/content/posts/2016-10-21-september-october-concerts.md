<p><b>16 October –<br />
Sussex Jazz Kings</b></p>
<p>This group was well received and it&rsquo;s New Orleans-style jazz<br />
in a range of styles went down well.  The<br />
band consisted of Dave Stradwick on cornet, Bernard Stuit on clarinet, Iain<br />
McAulay on trombone, Pete Lay on drugs, Phil Durell on banjo and Peter Clancy<br />
on double bass and sousaphone.  A<br />
beautiful sound – we hope it&rsquo;s not too long before they are back with us.</p>
<figure data-orig-width="899" data-orig-height="674" class="tmblr-full"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_ofed7nDNP01rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-width="899" data-orig-height="674" /></figure>
<p><b>9 October – Fret<br />
&amp; Fiddle present &ldquo;Grappelli Swing&rdquo;</b></p>
<p>What can I say!  One<br />
of the best concerts this year.  Fret<br />
&amp; Fiddle have expanded to include a drummer (Steve Street) and a keyboard<br />
player (Rich Hughes), taking the total of the group to five.  Saline, such a talented violinist, narrated<br />
the story of Grappelli&rsquo;s life, his involvement and long-time work with Django<br />
Reinhardt, and played his music through the decades.  The whole show was informative, the music a<br />
delight and intense, and I hope we see them again soon.</p>
<figure data-orig-width="906" data-orig-height="679" class="tmblr-full"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_ofed8dCAOM1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-width="906" data-orig-height="679" /></figure>
<p><b>2 October – Spats<br />
Langham&rsquo;s Hot Fingers Jazz Band</b></p>
<p>Something happened at tonight&rsquo;s concert.  Three musicians melted our hearts with a mix<br />
of music from the 20s and 30s, music moreover not routinely heard.  Miss Emily Campbell, vocalist, with the<br />
nickname of &lsquo;The Cotswold Canary&rsquo;, joined in.<br />
Spats talked knowledgeably about the music the trio played so, like Fret<br />
&amp; Fiddle the show was also educational.<br />
Malcolm Sked played tuba and double bass and Daniel Blythe was<br />
multi-instrumental – indeed playing one of my own personal favourites which is<br />
the harmonica.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="679" data-orig-width="905"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_ofed9gF7351rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="679" data-orig-width="905" /></figure>
<p></p>
<p>During the interval of this show Chris and Joan Latham<br />
presented Spats with his award, Musician of the Year, which he gained at the<br />
last concert in May 2016.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="666" data-orig-width="888"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_ofeda87LCH1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="666" data-orig-width="888" /></figure>
<p><b>25 September –<br />
Brian Carrick&rsquo;s Algiers Stompers</b></p>
<p>Listening to this band it was all too easy to imagine<br />
yourself in a dance hall in New Orleans in the first half of the 20th<br />
century.  Brian Carrick, as always,<br />
supplied a steady stream of humour.  It<br />
should be noted that he played on the clarinet6 formerly owned by George<br />
Lewis.  Along with Brian were Peter<br />
Wright (trumpet), Chas Hudson (trombone), ‘Mac&rsquo; MacDonald (banjo, Andrew Hall<br />
(piano), Bill Cole (bass) and Ray Bowden (drums).  What a night!</p>
<p><b>18 September – Old<br />
Fashioned Love Band</b></p>
<p>Again a blast of harmonica (is it catching on again I<br />
wonder?) from Bill Smith who also played the trumpet and sang. Mike Pemberton<br />
was on the trombone, Jake Reeves on banjo and guitar, Harri Forelius on double<br />
bass and Moe Green on drums.  How does<br />
Nick Balmforth do it as we listen to one stunning performance after another.</p>
<p><b>11 September –<br />
Tame Valley Stompers</b></p>
<p>Sadly I missed this one due to being in Southport to hear<br />
the Pasadena Roof Orchestra (and yes they were fabulous) and accompanying<br />
bands.  However everyone I have spoken to<br />
have said that the Stompers were as on form as ever.  Sorry Terry (trombone), Roger (trumpet), Paul<br />
(reeds), Pete (bass guitar), John (banjo and guitar) and Norman on drums.  However I am sure you will be back and I will<br />
make sure I am around for that gig.</p>
<p><b>4 September –<br />
Phoenix Jazzmen with Sheila Fawkes</b></p>
<p>This was a smashing start to the new season for Stafford<br />
Jazz Society.  With drummer Ken Wright on<br />
holiday we had Andy Lowe in his place.<br />
Sheila Fawkes was in fine voice and as in the past we all enjoyed her<br />
singing.  Pete Robinson told a beautiful<br />
story about the first time China Boy was played.  A Chinese man took offence and reported the<br />
player to the police for racial abuse.<br />
The police arrested the banjo player … in a Chinese restaurant!!  Fortunately common sense prevailed and no<br />
further action was taken.  It is these<br />
little reminiscences which make sessions so enjoyable.</p>
<p>Sorry there are no pictures for September folks – I thought<br />
I had taken some but … early senility?!?!</p>
