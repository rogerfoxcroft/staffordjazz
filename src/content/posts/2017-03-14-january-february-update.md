<p>19 February – Annie Hawkins&rsquo; Funky Butt Hall Jazzmen.  Sadly Annie was unable to come along on this<br />
occasion due to a nasty chest infection.<br />
In her place came Isabel Toner.<br />
Joining her were Peter Wright on cornet, Jeff Milner (tailgate<br />
trombone), Karl Hird (reeds), Malcolm Hogarth (piano), Bob Rowbottom (banjo)<br />
and Barry Wood on drums.  The second set,<br />
in particular, reached out to all in the room.<br />
We hope Annie recovers soon and look forward to seeing this group again<br />
in the future.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="517" data-orig-width="693"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_omtbuzo5mq1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="517" data-orig-width="693" /></figure>
<p>12 February – Baby Jools &amp; The Jazzoholics).  This was our first evening concert and what a<br />
concert!  Each and every musician is so<br />
talented from Baby Jools on drums to Jim Swinnerton on double bass.  Joining them were Denny Ilett (trumpet), Mike<br />
Owen (Trombone) and Brian Mellor (banjo and guitar).  The music was mixed and marvellous.  It was impossible for anyone to sit still and<br />
there was such a buzz in the room – electric.
</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="795" data-orig-width="647"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_omtbv6BLbZ1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="795" data-orig-width="647" /></figure>
<p>5 February – Savannah Jazz Band.  Unfortunately due to a chest infection I had<br />
to miss this show.  I am reliably<br />
informed that, as always, this band &ldquo;produced the goods&rdquo;.  The players were Bill Smith (trumpet &amp;<br />
harmonica), Brian Ellis (trombone &amp; piano), Roger Myerscough (reeds), Chris<br />
Marney (banjo), Tony Pollitt (double bass) and John Meehan (drums).  Always a welcome band for Stafford Jazz<br />
Society.</p>
<p>29 January – Merseysippi Jazz Band.  I think it fair to say that clarinetist Dave<br />
Dixson had by far the best voice so shame he only sang the one number.  More next time please.  This is the longest established band in the<br />
country.  An interesting addition to<br />
today&rsquo;s concert was the mounted mirror devised by one of our members, Chris<br />
Latham, which enabled the pianist – Malcolm Hogarth – to see the rest of the<br />
band who were playing behind him.<br />
Ingenious device – hope Chris has taken out a patent on it!  The remainder of the band were John Higham on<br />
trumpet, Peter Fryer (trombone), Dave Dixson (clarinet), Isabel Toner (double<br />
bass) and Pete Darwin on drums.   As a<br />
previous winner of BBC&rsquo;s Jazz Heritage Award you know you have something<br />
special when you get this band.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="516" data-orig-width="696"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_omtbveQGuM1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="516" data-orig-width="696" /></figure>
<p>22 January – Stafford Grammar School Big Band.  This band received their SJS Band of the Year<br />
Award in amazement.  Today&rsquo;s programme<br />
was a mixture and I am absolutely delighted to say that a piece I heard for the<br />
first time when they last played for us – Children of Sanchez (composed by<br />
Chuck Mangione) – was played again with trumpet solo by Gavin Lamplaugh, the<br />
band&rsquo;s musical director.  This is such an<br />
emotional piece of music and if you have never heard it track it down – it&rsquo;s<br />
wonderful.  This band continues to make<br />
its mark on the music scene.</p>

<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="729" data-orig-width="559"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_omtbvqDbqo1rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="729" data-orig-width="559" /></figure>
<p>15 January – Jake Leg Jug Band with Mike Pembroke.  Firstly a big apology to Mike who for months<br />
I have been mis-naming as Mike Pemberton. So sorry Mike – it&rsquo;s probably down to<br />
my age!  The band has a new singer, Sarah<br />
Miller, an attractive young woman with a lovely voice.  JLJB are folksy in the nicest possible way<br />
and received rapturous applause at the end of each number.  Doubtless whatever genre this music falls<br />
into JLJB have got it right.  The pace<br />
changed frequently but the quality remained high.  I simply loved this concert.  The band are:<br />
Duncan Wilcox (double bass), Neil Hulse (guitar), Toby Wilson (banjo<br />
&amp; dobro) and Liam Ward (harmonica &amp; jug).  I cannot wait for them to return.</p>
<figure class="tmblr-full" data-orig-height="537" data-orig-width="717"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/tumblr/tumblr_inline_omtbw0HVX91rp3l2g_540.webp" data-orig-height="537" data-orig-width="717" /></figure>
<p>8 January – Jon Critchley&rsquo;s Original Panama Jazz Band.  This band welcomed SJS back after it&rsquo;s<br />
Christmas break.   They played a variety<br />
of music and we had nice solos by both pianist (Malcolm Hogarth) and<br />
clarinetist (Dave Thomas).  Additional<br />
members of the band are Jon Critchley on trumpet, Laurie Cooper on trombone,<br />
Isabel Toner on string bass and Bill Buck on drums.  During the show&rsquo;s interval there was a<br />
presentation to one of the SJS members, Heather Mansell, of her very own brolly<br />
as a reward for the enthusiasm she shows during the brolly parade.</p>
